🍵 Tablecloth Trick: Whip a silk tablecloth out from under Mum's bowls. Fast, or everything goes with it.

A family table laid with a silk cloth: a tea cup, Mum's bowls, a whole braised fish, Grandma's thermos and the family heirloom. Grab the hem and flick it down. Fast enough and the cloth slides out while everything rattles and stays; too slow and the whole table rides over the edge. Six tables, each harder than the last, and a cat. A tiny cloth-physics toy, no sign-up.

How to play

  1. Put a finger (or the mouse) on the hem of the cloth hanging over the near edge of the table.
  2. Flick it down hard and let go. The faster the flick, the less the things on the table move. A slow drag takes everything with it.
  3. Perfect means the cloth is off, nothing broke and nothing slid more than 3 cm. Then the next table is laid, with more on it.
  4. Table six has the family heirloom on it. A firm pull is not enough there.

FAQ

Does the tablecloth trick really work?

Yes, and this toy simulates why. Friction can only push a dish so hard, so if the cloth leaves quickly enough the dish only gets a short nudge and barely moves. A slow pull gives friction time to drag it along. Silk helps because it is slippery.

Why did everything fall off?

The pull was too slow. Below about one metre a second the dishes ride the cloth all the way over the edge. Flick it.

Why does the heirloom fall over when the bowls are fine?

It is tall and narrow, so the same tug that slides a bowl a little tips the vase. It needs a faster pull than anything else on the table.

Is the cat part of the trick?

The cat does not move. The cloth leaves from under it. Sometimes, afterwards, it pushes a cup off the table anyway; tap the cup to stop it.
